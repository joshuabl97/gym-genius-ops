# Gym Genius: Strategic Questions

Deep business planning questions to strengthen the pre-investment action plan.

**Strategy:** EU-first (Equipment Utilization), WT-secondary (Workout Tracking)

---

## Vision & Positioning

### Q1: Why gyms?
You could apply this tech to home fitness, physical therapy clinics, sports training facilities, or corporate wellness. What makes commercial gyms the right wedge? Is it market size, access, or something else?

**Your Answer:**
- Gyms need operational data but don't have it—equipment purchasing is gut-feel
- Equipment utilization is a clear B2B value prop with measurable ROI
- Personal passion: I love fitness, love tracking workouts, and want to build complex AI technology
- EU is simpler to sell (B2B, operational value) than WT (requires member adoption)

**Primary value prop for first 10 gyms:** Know which equipment is used, when, and by how many people.

**Long-term vision:** EU for revenue, WT as premium upsell for member differentiation.

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU demo is simpler—just needs zone detection working reliably
- Focus sales conversations on equipment ROI, not member features
- WT becomes the "and there's more coming" story

---

### Q2: Who is the actual customer?
The plan references gym owners, but within a gym there are owners, managers, trainers, and members. Who writes the check? Who champions the purchase internally? Who uses it daily? Are these the same person?

**Your Answer (EU Focus):**
- **Pitch to:** Gym owners/operators (they write the check)
- **Champion:** Operations manager or facilities manager
- **Daily user:** Management team reviewing utilization data
- **Members:** Not involved in EU product (no member-facing features)

**EU is simpler:** No trainer politics, no member adoption curve, just operational data for decision-makers.

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU sales cycle is cleaner—one decision-maker
- No need to convince trainers or members
- Focus on operational/financial value, not member experience

---

### Q3: What business are you really in?
Is Gym Genius a:
- Hardware company that sells camera/compute bundles?
- SaaS company that licenses software?
- Data company that monetizes workout insights?
- Platform that connects gyms, trainers, and members?

Each implies a different valuation multiple, growth trajectory, and competitive moat.

**Your Answer:**

**EU Track:** B2B SaaS with hardware component
- Gyms pay hardware lease + software subscription
- Simple per-gym pricing
- No platform complexity
- Data stays with the gym

**WT Track (Future):** Platform + Data hybrid (like Strava for strength training)
- Members have accounts that work across gyms
- Social features, data portability
- Premium member tiers

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU is simpler to build and sell—focus here first
- WT platform complexity can wait until EU is generating revenue
- Valuation story: "B2B SaaS now, platform upside later"

### Q7: Why would a gym trust a camera-based system?
Privacy concerns are real. Some members actively avoid gyms with cameras. How do you address this? Is consent opt-in? What data is stored vs. discarded?

**Your Answer (EU Focus):**

EU privacy is MUCH simpler than WT:

- **No video stored** — Only aggregate occupancy data
- **No member consent needed** — Not tracking individuals

**Implications for Plan:**

## Business Model

### Q8: What's the deployment cost vs. recurring revenue?
You mention hardware potentially costing <$2k—who absorbs that cost? If the gym pays upfront, your sales cycle lengthens. If you eat it, your burn rate increases dramatically.

**Your Answer (EU Model):**

**Hardware lease + subscription model:**
- Gym pays monthly hardware lease (~$200-300/mo)
- Gym pays software subscription (~$300-500/mo)
- Total: ~$500-800/mo per gym
- Lower barrier than big upfront cost

**EU hardware is simpler:**
- Fewer cameras needed (just need zone coverage)
- Standard edge compute box
- Easier installation than WT (no precise angle requirements)

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU hardware cost is lower than WT would be
- Faster payback on hardware investment
- Can start with 2-3 cameras per gym

---

### Q9: What's a gym worth to you annually?
If you charge $X/month, what does your unit economics look like? Hardware amortization, support costs, churn risk?

**Your Answer (EU Model):**

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| Monthly revenue | $500-800 |
| Annual revenue | $6,000-9,600 |
| Hardware cost | ~$1,500-2,000 |
| Hardware payback | 3-4 months |
| Support cost/gym | ~$50/mo (remote) |
| Gross margin | ~70-80% after hardware payback |

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU unit economics are attractive
- Need 10 gyms for ~$60-100k ARR
- Focus on retention—churn kills the model

## Competition & Defensibility

### Q11: What happens when Peloton, Apple, or Google enters this space?
They have the capital, distribution, and brand trust. What's your moat?

**Your Answer (EU Focus):**

"They could, but they won't prioritize it because gym equipment utilization is too niche, and by the time they do, we'll have gym relationships and vertical expertise."

**EU-Specific Moats:**
- **Vertical focus (Medium):** Big cos chase bigger markets
- **Gym relationships (Strong over time):** Trust builds with each successful deployment
- **Speed (Medium):** We ship gym-specific features faster
- **Data flywheel (Strong if scaled):** More gyms = better benchmarking data

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU niche is defensible—too small for big players
- Build gym relationships now
- Create switching costs via data history

---

### Q12: Why hasn't this been done already?
The components (pose estimation, object detection) are available. What's the insight that makes this possible *now* that wasn't possible 3 years ago?

**Your Answer:**
1. **Tech cost reduced** — Edge compute and cameras now affordable
2. **AI accuracy crossed threshold** — Detection reliable enough for production
3. **Big players focused elsewhere** — Enterprise CV companies chasing retail, warehouse, not gyms

**EU-Specific:** Zone occupancy is even simpler than workout tracking—the tech has been ready longer, but nobody focused on gyms.

**Implications for Plan:**
- "Why now" is strong for EU
- Simpler tech = faster to market

### Q13: What do Tempo, Tonal, and Mirror teach you?
They raised hundreds of millions but some have struggled. What's the lesson?

**Your Answer:**
- **B2C hardware is hard** — Consumer fickleness, high CAC, hardware margins
- **We're B2B** — Gym pays, not consumer
- **We're not selling hardware** — Hardware is enabler, software is product
- **EU is even safer** — No member adoption curve, just operational value

**Implications for Plan:**
- EU avoids their mistakes
- B2B + operational value = more defensible

### Q29: What's the minimum viable EU product?

**MVP Features:**
1. Zone configuration UI (draw zones on video)
2. Zone occupancy detection (occupied/empty)
3. Utilization dashboard (% time used, peak hours)
4. Historical reports (weekly summaries)

**Not MVP:**
- Real-time alerts
- Multi-location comparison
- API integrations
- Member-facing anything (that's WT)
