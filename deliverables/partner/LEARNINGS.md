## EU Pain Points That Resonate
- Gyms don't know which equipment is actually used vs. just taking up space
- Peak hour crowding is visible but not quantified
- Equipment purchasing decisions are based on gut feel, not data

## Effective EU Pitches
- "We track zones, not machines" - simpler, cheaper than per-machine sensors
- "Use your existing cameras" - reduces friction and upfront cost
- "Know before you buy" - data-driven equipment ROI

## EU Objection Handling
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Too many cameras needed" | Most gyms only need 3-4 cameras for full zone coverage |
| "We already have cameras" | Great! We can often use existing infrastructure |
| "Seems complicated" | One box, connects to cameras, data in days |

## Camera Coverage Insights (TKT-29)
- Wide-angle cameras (2.8mm, 110° FOV) cover ~800-1,200 sq ft each
- Optimal mounting height: 9-12 feet
- Corner mounting maximizes coverage
- Most gyms can reuse existing security cameras (2018+ IP cameras)
- Zone-based approach means fewer cameras than machine-level tracking

## Deployment Cost Insights (TKT-21)
- Year 1 total: $6,700-9,000 (existing cameras) or $7,950-10,850 (new cameras)
- Per-machine IoT alternative costs $8,000-17,000/year for 50 machines
- Manual tracking labor costs ~$9,500/year (1.5 hrs/day at $17.50/hr)
- Conservative payback: 6-12 months
- Biggest ROI: avoiding one bad equipment purchase ($10K-50K savings)

## ROI Angles That Work
- "Avoid buying equipment you don't need" - resonates with budget-conscious owners
- "Use your existing cameras" - reduces perceived complexity and cost
- "Zone data is enough for purchasing decisions" - justifies simpler approach vs per-machine
- "Pay for itself in one avoided bad purchase" - concrete, believable value
